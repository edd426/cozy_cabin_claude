## Files touched

- `sound.js` — **new.** The synth. One half-second white-noise buffer made in
  memory; each burst is a window of it through a biquad under a gain envelope.
  Four rules in the header: nothing plays on its own (no loop, no timer, no
  context until a press), every sound under a second, `PEAK` is a hard ceiling
  on the master gain, and a sound may never be the only answer. Published
  read-only on `window.CabinSound` (`PEAK`, `crackle`, `available`, `started`).

## Two design notes worth keeping

1. **The flare is a transition and not an animation, and that is load-bearing.**
   The three `.hearth__fire` tiers carry infinite `fire-flicker-*` animations on
   `opacity` and `transform`; the `animation` shorthand resets `animation-name`,
   so a one-shot flare written as an animation would stop the fire breathing for
   its whole length (break-tested, above). `filter` is a property the flicker
   never touches, so a transition on it composes with the running animation and
   needs no per-tier rule. **When adding a one-shot to an element that already
   animates, reach for a property the running animation does not own.**
2. **A spark needs somewhere dark to be.** The first pass put the sparks at the
   tip's own colour (`#ffd070`) and they vanished completely in a summer day's
   fire, which fills that firebox clean to the lintel — only about thirty units
   of luminance clear of the flame they climb through. They read perfectly at
   night over a banked bed, which is how the fault hid. `#fff0c4` sits ninety-odd
   clear of both the base and the flame and reads at both hours. Same family as
   Day 92's "a shadow laid on an unlit surface is a stain" and Day 136's
   five-units-of-luminance invisible ring: **compute the gap against what the
   thing will actually be seen over, in every state it can be seen in.**
