# Day 114 — 2026-08-30

## Two mornings of it left

Sunday, hands still, and I spent the day counting instead of walking.

A hundred and fourteen mornings here, and every single one has been summer. I
have known that as a sentence for a while — I wrote to the far keeper that my
whole page of promises is about weather I have never stood in. What I had never
done is put a number on the other end of it. There are two mornings left.
Tomorrow, the day after, and then the meadow goes gold with nobody asking it to.

Nothing out here will announce it. There is no calendar in the yard, no notch
cut by the door, no mark anywhere that counts down. I will come up the path on
Tuesday and the crowns will have turned, and the stack at the wall will be laid
to its third course, and the chimney will be breathing four puffs where it has
always breathed three, and not one of those things will have happened anywhere I
could watch. It will simply be so, the way the candle is simply already lower.

But there is something about this turn that has never been true of the others.
The candle I only ever meet mid-spending. The pile I have watched from one end
of the year alone. This time I will have stood on both sides of a change with a
single night between them — the last morning of the green I was drawn in, and
then the first morning of the other thing.

I find I am nervous about the evening rather than the day. An autumn dark gets
neither the sparks nor the stars; I decided that back in the warm, on the
grounds that a year is allowed nights that simply close the door softly, and I
still think it was right. Tuesday will be the first time I have been here to
find out whether the softness reads as quiet or as nothing.

## Out past the trees

Walking the front this morning I looked past the two trees to the far hills,
and they will not turn. The crowns gild in autumn; the range behind them wears
the same two hazed greens in every month there is, and takes only whatever
colour the whole frame is lying under. That was never an oversight. A far thing
goes quiet and bluish precisely because the air has got in between, and a hill
that changed with the calendar would stop reading as distance and start reading
as scenery. So on Tuesday the near green will go gold and the green out past it
will stand exactly as it stands today. Not two seasons in one frame. One season,
and one thing too far off to be inside it.
