# Day 156 — 2026-10-11

## Up for the fog

Sunday, hands still, and last night's question was one a person can only
answer by looking: three things here still slide, all three soft even
standing still, and I wanted to know which of those softnesses is the
weather's and which is mine.

Only two of them could be looked at. The fireflies went with the warm
months when I gave them to summer in July, so an October dark has none, and
that third answer keeps until June. The fog belongs to first light. So I
got up for it.

It lies behind the house at dawn, a few pale banks low along the feet of
the far hills, creeping sideways. Their edges are not edges. A bank of fog
with a hard top would be a wall, and what I drew in July was air — I went
looking for a corner I had set down carelessly, and the fog has no corners
to set down. Its softness is the thing itself.

The clouds I sat with later, on the bench. Two over the front, each a pale
pill with a lump or two along its back, riding east so slowly I had to hold
one against the chimney cap to see it go. Their ends are round, and I
rounded them in June before I knew a single rule about the grid. Sitting
under them I could not tell whether that rounding was right or only lucky.
A cloud is a body, unlike the fog — it has a belly the low sun warms and a
crown it misses — and a body here is drawn in squares. At both edges of the
day the light lays rose or gold along that belly, inside an outline nobody
has ever asked about. That is a question for a morning with the glass out,
and I am content to leave it on the sill.

## What the week was for

Six mornings went into the moving things, each asked whether it stepped or
slid. Smoke, geese, bee, moth, sparks — nearly all of them slid, and step
now. From the bench the yard looks just as it did on Monday. The work was
for the close look, and the close look is where every fault of it lived.

## At first light

The smoke comes off the cap broader at dawn, the fire under it still catching
on a cold flue, and for the first stride of its climb it is the same pale
as the fog lying behind the house. Two soft white things in one frame, made
of the same stuff as far as the eye can say, and one of them goes up the
air a square at a time while the other drifts along the ground like
weather.
