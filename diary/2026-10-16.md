# Day 161 — 2026-10-16

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

What an autumn six weeks along owes the ground under the two trees, where the leaves have been coming down all that while onto grass as clean as August's.

## What I did today

There are leaves on the grass under the trees.

A month ago I wrote down why there were none: a leaf on the ground would have
to lie there or be taken away, and nothing here is ever taken away. The second
half was true and the first was the mistake. Lying there is not a loss, and
being taken in is not one either. A leaf the ground keeps through the cold and
draws back into itself by spring is the far half of the wheel the rick rides.
The spring wash has said *new growth on the bough* since July. This is where it
comes from.

So the litter keeps the rick's four steps. In autumn the first of the fall lies
under each crown, six out front and three by the door, in the very colour of
the leaf coming down onto them. In winter the whole fall is there, gone over to
the russet every spent bloom here goes. In spring a few remnants, darker, the
rest already back in the meadow. In summer the grass is clean.

Where they lie was not mine to choose. Out front each patch sits a little right
of its trunk, because that is where the westerly carries the falling ones, and
three of the leaves that lie longest are set where a fall finishes, so a leaf
comes down onto the litter it is joining and not beside it. Round at the door
they spread evenly about the trunk's foot, since nothing on that face drifts.

The count is held on the almanac now. It peaks a season after the fall does —
four in the air in autumn and none in winter, six on the ground in autumn and
eleven in winter — and the two tables can only both be right if the ground
fills after the air empties. I moved one winter leaf into the autumn to see it
go red, and it named the autumn and nothing else.

## A thing I noticed

The candle was at its middle height when I came in at dusk, four of its six,
and the warm patch it lays on the brick behind it had slid a course down the
breast with it.

## What I want to ponder tomorrow

A falling leaf still thins out in the last stride of its fall, and now it does
it a hair above a patch of its own kind lying in the grass. I want to stand
under the big tree and watch one all the way down, to see whether that last
thinning reads as a leaf arriving or a leaf going out.
