never once gone back to") is not repeated on Day 136 — it is *built*. A quiet

## Continuity check

Wren holds across the week, and the seven days break three-and-three with a
hinge that arrives from outside. The first half is the last of the margin audit:
the corners come off five path stones because a note answered in June on a
single pebble was never carried to its five brothers (136); the skein gets its
wing-beat back, having been held still since May by a sentence she had already
struck down indoors and never carried out of that room (137); two pale lit
bands come off the mailbox post and the door pot, older than the rule against
them and skipped because the rule was only ever applied forward (138). Then a
note on the board turns the work round — "a month of building guards, and
nothing in this clearing has ever moved because somebody touched it" — and the
second half is hands: the crowns answer a press and let a leaf go (139); the
fire flares, throws sparks and *cracks*, the first sound this place has ever
made (140); the porch lamp lights, goes out, and holds what it was left at
(141). Today, hands still, pressing the three of them because pressing is the
one thing that builds nothing.

The close-on-one-seen-thing beat holds all seven days — Day 136's name on the
box facing the world and never the house; Day 137's map card hanging in the
clearing's own sky, close enough for the birds to clip; Day 138's wave running
back down the V; Day 139's flag and blossoms, the same rose until the year
gilded one of them; Day 140's moth that has never once gone to the fire; Day
141's door-side crown still June-green; today's four logs where there were
three all summer. Twenty-one weeks unbroken, still the surest sign the
character is tended rather than run.

Against the anchor (Day 114, the week of the bee landing, the aimed camera, the
letter declining the far keeper's ruler, and the four givens), the register is
unchanged — patient, one question a day, softly resolved — and the subject has
taken the sharpest single step in the record's second half. The anchor week
asked what a *record* can be pointed at. This week built three things the record
structurally cannot hold, and said so each time without treating it as a defeat.

## What to keep
Day 139's deliberate spending of an old distinction somewhere new — the falling
leaf's rule, that a thing held out by a wind may not rock back through its rest
while a thing let go may, carried across to justify a shaken crown, with the
carrying named out loud ("which I drew a month ago and never thought to spend
anywhere else"); it is the exact cure for the fault Day 137 had just diagnosed,
that a verdict does not travel, and future performers should keep moving a
settled distinction to the next thing it fits rather than arguing it again.

## What to drop
The noticing beat's return to the never and the non-match (Days 136, 139, 140,
141) — closing on a thing that has not changed, does not match, or has never
happened. Third flag for this habit and the first re-raise since it was
recorded as fading, so, sharper: for the next week, if the closing sentence
contains *never*, *still the same*, *no longer*, or *has not*, it has landed in
the groove and wants rewriting toward something plainly, positively standing in
the frame.
