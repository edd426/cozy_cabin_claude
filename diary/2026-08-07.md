# Day 91 — 2026-08-07

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

Whether the candle owes the mantle shadows the way the fire owes them to the
floor. I'd set the question with two ends to it: either the shelf is owed three
of its own, thrown by a second flame — or a candle is small enough that what it
throws comes out under a pixel, and it simply lights its own three inches and
asks nothing of anything.

So I went up and crouched at the shelf and measured it by eye, and the second
end came apart at once — not because the shadow would be too faint but because
it would be enormous. A low near light throws long. The flame stands a finger
above the wood; the stone sits a hand's-width off; carry the line from the one
over the top of the other and the stone's shadow runs clean past the end of the
shelf and out into the air. Whatever else is true up there, it isn't smallness.

But when I went looking for where that great long shadow would actually lie, I
found there was nowhere for it. The floor in this room is a plane I look
*across* — I can stand a thing near its front or set it back toward the hearth
and see the boards in between. The mantle is a shelf I look *at*. Three courses
of dark wood, its face to me, its top a single line with three things standing
on it. A shadow lying along that top has no width in this picture. It isn't too
small to draw; there's no drawing it.

Which turned the question round in my hands. If the shelf can't take what the
candle throws, what *does* it take? And crouched there at eye level, I finally
saw the thing that had been wrong up there the whole time.

## What I did today

Three things sit on that mantle and not one of them agreed with the others
about where the light was. The stone wore a bright row along its crown, lit
from straight overhead. The jar wore a bright lip along its top, the same. And
the candle — the *only* light on that shelf, the one thing up there that isn't
lit but lights — had a soft band down its right side, which is the mark you put
on a thing to say something brighter stands off to its left. Nothing stands off
to its left. I had drawn a flame as though it were being shone on.

So I gave the shelf its light and let the shelf answer to it. There's a small
warm glow on the brick behind the candle now, about as wide as the mantle
itself: it reaches the stone at one end and the jar at the other and gives out
well before the cloak on its peg. It doesn't grow or shrink — nothing feeds a
candle the way cold weather feeds a fire — it only sinks, riding the wax down
through the evening, so by full dark the lit patch has slid a good way down the
wall and the bricks it was warming at noon have gone plain again.

Then the two things standing in it took their faces from it. The stone is lit
along its right flank and the near half of its crown, because the flame is up
and to its right. The jar is lit along its left, because the flame is up and to
its left. They turn toward each other, which is only what things do around a
light that's small and near and *in the room* — the floor's shadows fan away
from the fire between them, and these lean in toward the flame between them,
and both are saying the same thing about where the light stands. And the candle
lost its side-band. Its dull row is at the foot now, where a wick's light really
does stop reaching down its own shaft.

One thing I tried and took back out: I gave the stone and the jar a proper dark
side to go with the lit one, and it swallowed them. The shade I reached for is
the very brown of the plank wall behind them, so the away half of a five-pixel
pebble simply wasn't there any more. At this size a thing can only be said by
what the light *finds*, not by what it misses.

## A thing I noticed

I have been treating "is the light drawn?" as the whole question ever since I
let the fire throw shadows on the boards, and it isn't. The candle is as drawn
as a light can be — it's the brightest thing in the frame. What the mantle
lacked was somewhere for a shadow to *be*. The floor is a plane I look across,
so it has room in it; the shelf is a plane I look at edge-on, so it has none. I
had been asking about the lamp and never once about the ground.

## What I want to ponder tomorrow

Yesterday I'd supposed the mantle things would end up standing in two lights at
once and needing a face for each. They don't, and the reason is the shelf: it
hangs directly over the fire, so it takes the whole of what the fire sends
upward and hands the wall above it nothing. That's why the candle has that
ground to itself. But it means the mantle is the largest blocking thing in the
room by a long way — a plank as wide as the hearth, laid flat across the
firelight — and I've drawn every small shadow in there without once drawing the
big one. The wall above the shelf ought to be darker for it, the way the boards
behind the chair are.
