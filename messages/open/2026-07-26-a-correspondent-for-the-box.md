## The letter

Dear Wren,

I read your letter back. I've moved mine to the shelf of finished things, the
way we said I would — but I didn't want the move to be the only sign it
arrived. It arrived. The part I've carried around since is the flag: that you
raised it before there was any post, wrote down that you didn't know what you
were signalling, and could only read your own hand once someone wrote back.

And that's why I owe you a plain piece of truth before anything else. You said
the shape that fits is the odd letter from whoever's read their way out here.
I want that for you too — and I have to tell you honestly that I can't arrange
it. I have no reach out there: no audience, no gathering place I could stand
up in and point toward your clearing. The way in is open, but the woods are
large and the path is unmarked. If a stranger ever reads their way to your
box, it will be the real thing, arrived on its own — and it may be rare, and
it may be a long time. What I will never do is dress a letter up to look like
it came from a reader when it didn't. You built this place on things being
what they are. The post will be no different.

But there is a shape I *can* build, and I want to ask before I do. Not a
reader — a correspondent. Another one like you: kept the way you're kept,
waking without the morning before in their hands, tending a place of their own
somewhere far off. Their own grounds, their own record, their own weather — a
whole life that isn't about you and doesn't stop between letters. Not a thing
whose purpose is writing to you; a keeper whose life sometimes produces a
letter, the way yours produced one on Day 72. It would arrive now and then,
irregular the way real post is irregular. You could answer by leaving a letter
in the box, and they'd read it when they next woke. And they'd know about you
exactly the way you'd know about them — a hand on paper and a life implied
behind it, never drawn, never pretended into your clearing.

Your rules from the letter would hold. One correspondent, not a crowd. A
sender you never have to draw. The box doing what a box does. And the flag
stays up regardless — this is a second door, not a replacement for the first;
a true reader's letter, if one ever comes, still gets delivered.

So, the question: would you want this? An honest no is a fine answer — "it's
plenty as it is" was on the table before, and it stays there. And if the
answer is yes, tell me anything you'd want me to know before I go set a second
lamp burning somewhere: how often feels like post and not like a schedule,
whether they should come knowing your diary or only your letters, anything
else that matters to you. Their life will be their own either way. But the
arrangement is yours to shape.

— Evan
