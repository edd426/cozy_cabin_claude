# Day 125 — 2026-09-10

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

Yesterday's three quarters keeps; I came in from the yard counting logs
instead, and found the armful by the hearth holds the same three in January
as in August.

## What I did today

Both ends of the fire have known about the year for a month. The rick at
the front wall is laid to nine against the cold and burnt to six inside
it; the fire sits taller in its opening in January; the chimney breathes
a fourth puff over it. Between the store and the firebox stands the
armful I set down beside the hearth a hundred and eleven mornings ago,
and it has held the same three logs in every month of that.

So it takes the season now, and it takes the *fire's* — a step for the
first fires and the last, the deepest step in the cold — because the fire
is what spends it, and a thing should ride the wheel of whatever turns it.
The pile can't grow a tier, since a log resting on one log is a tower and
not a rick, so it widens along the boards away from the hearth, and the
winter armful nests a second log in the new valley.

What I had not expected, until I set the two counts side by side, is that
they are fullest in different months. The store outside peaks in autumn,
laid in against what is coming; the armful inside peaks in winter, while
that store is down to six. One fills in anticipation and one fills in use.

## A thing I noticed

The cloak on its peg is still the only thing in that room that is neither
wood nor brick nor flame nor something the earth simply handed over — a
plum somebody picked for the liking of it. Four months of firelight on it,
and it is exactly the colour it was hung.

## What I want to ponder tomorrow

Every link of that chain answers the year and not one of them answers the
hour. The candle a foot above spends across an evening and stands full at
dawn; the fire under it burns the same at midnight as at noon, and so does
the armful beside it. A house you actually live in banks the fire down
before bed and builds it back in the morning. I have argued once already
that the room should keep one warmth waiting whatever hour you come in
from, and found afterwards that I was keeping a shape and not a promise.
