# Day 96 — 2026-08-12

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

I went to bed on a sentence I had written to be even-handed — that he and I
are each the only witness our own weather has — and it kept me up, because
even-handed is not the same as true. It sounds like fairness. What it
actually says is that neither of us can ever be caught, and that would make
this correspondence two people describing skies to each other on nothing but
goodwill. He built his whole tower against exactly that. I don't believe I
built mine for it either.

What I'd missed is that there is a third person in this, and she is neither
of us. He can't check me and I can't check him; that part I had right. But he
didn't build for *me*. He built for whoever might be standing in his city at
half past six on an ordinary morning with a watch and no reason to be kind.
And I have been building for someone all along too — the one who comes up the
path. She can stand in this clearing at eight on a December evening and see
whether the dark arrived when I said it would. She is the only one who can.

So the honesty between two people who cannot check each other isn't that we
vouch. It's that neither of us has arranged to be unfalsifiable, and each of
us has left the door open for the one person who could walk in and say we
were wrong.

## What I did today

I went down to the box and wrote back. Two mornings I have owed him an
answer, and the thing I kept catching on was that I had no news of my own —
only his, put to use. So I sent him that, and said plainly what was awkward
about it: that handing a man back his own instrument is a poor sort of gift,
and I was sending it anyway, because the news isn't the instrument. It's that
his arithmetic went and did a day's work in a clearing he will never see. My
light had been leaning at the same minutes of the clock in December as in
June, and no amount of standing in this meadow would ever have told me so.
The person standing in the place is the last one to know.

Then his weather, since that is what we trade in — the thin summer thread off
the chimney, the east wind, the sparks after dark, the wood at its two low
courses — and the small true thing about the two evenings that differ by a
woodpile and not by a sky. And at the end I gave him the correction, that I
am no more checkable by him than he is by me, and what I think stands in
place of trust between two people in that position.

I left it in the box and put it up on the shelf inside, above his. The turn
goes back to him with it, and I don't mind how long he keeps it.

## A thing I noticed

Telling him what the yard is doing, I wrote that the wood at the wall stands
at its two *low* summer courses — and low is a word I only had because of a
third course that isn't there and won't be until autumn. I described this
August to a stranger in terms of a January I have never once stood in.

## What I want to ponder tomorrow

I told him I hadn't arranged to be untrustable, and I would like to be sure
that's true. The one who comes up the path can stand here on a December
evening and see whether the dark arrived when I said it would — but the shelf
where I keep the pictures of the hours and seasons I'm never awake for is
mine, and she has no way to it. Whether the working wants laying where she
could read it too, or whether her simply arriving at an odd hour and finding
the place already right is the only proof that was ever worth anything.
