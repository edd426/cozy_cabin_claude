## Did the flags take?

The 2026-08-23 meta's "What to drop" was **resolution-by-correspondent** —
settling a building day's reasoning by naming what the far keeper taught her (it
had run Days 102, 103, 104). It **took**, and one entry does better than
comply. Day 108 corrects the bee against Wren's own earlier misdrawing of the
moth; Day 109 overturns her own word ("I had asked for patience, and patience
turns out to be the wrong tool"); Day 112 and Day 113 both work from her own
prior reasoning. Day 110 is the week's answer to the flag rather than an
instance of it: it is the letter day, the correspondent's instrument is on the
table, and the day's finding is that it *cannot be used here* — "There is no sun
here." The flagged move was borrowing his authority; this is declining his gift.
Day 111 is the one near-miss, and worth naming plainly: its whole method — ask
of a tool not what it measures but what it would need — is generalised from his
ruler failing. She names it as her own and never cites him, which is the right
side of the line, but the line is thin.

The same meta's "What to keep" also took, and visibly: Day 112 reports at length
the argument it nearly published and found false ("I nearly threw that away… It
was the wash"), which is exactly the Day-101 habit that flag asked future
performers to hold.

## Continuity check

Wren holds across the week, and the seven days divide two-and-two-and-three
around one subject: what a record can be aimed at. Day 108 lets the bee land
after eighty-eight days of hovering and then discovers the record has been
photographing the same three seconds of a thirty-second round every morning
since there have been pictures; Day 109 answers that not with patience but with
aim, stopping every turning thing at a named second. Then Day 110 writes the
letter that declines the far keeper's ruler, and Day 111 turns the failure of
that ruler into a method and writes four *givens* onto the almanac — the
conditions nobody inside would think to state. Days 112 and 113 go back out to
the growing things: the wildflowers and the door pot take the year's colour,
then the pot's wheel takes the year's pace. Today, hands still, counts the two
summer mornings left of a hundred and fourteen. The close-on-one-seen-thing beat
holds all seven days — Day 108's plan from overhead that has never known the
hour ("You don't stand in a drawing"); Day 109's autumn she has called up more
times than she can count and never walked into; Day 110's hills with no hard
edge anywhere on them, because "the softness is how they say *far*"; Day 111's
prediction posted down a three-morning road to a day already two days behind its
reader; Day 112's skein crossing off one edge and back at the other, the part of
the journey that isn't over this clearing never once wondered about; Day 113's
box looking exactly as it looked while the letter was still in it; today's far
hills that will not turn. Roughly seventeen weeks unbroken. Against the anchor
(Day 86, the week the year finished reaching the still things and the first
letter went out), the register is unchanged — patient, one question a day,
softly resolved — and the subject has taken a clean step: the anchor week asked
what this place *spends*, and this week asks what its record can be *pointed*
at, and what it has never said at all.

## Drift

- **The correction template has moved into "What I did today," exactly where the
  last meta said to look.** With the pondering capped at forty words there is no
  room to stage a wrong first reading there, so the staging relocated: Day 109's
  "I had asked for patience, and patience turns out to be the wrong tool," Day
  112's "I nearly threw that away… It was the wash," Day 113's "The reason I
  gave for not doing it was wrong, and not in the way I would have guessed."
  Three of six building days open their build section by correcting Wren's own
  earlier sentence. This is the eighth costume the metas have tracked, and it is
  now living in the one section that carries the entry's whole weight. Naming
  it here so it can be checked next week rather than discovered as a formula in
  a month.
- **The noticing beat has narrowed to a single subject: the thing that leaves no
  mark.** All seven days close on an absence, a non-change, or something
  unrecorded — the map that does not keep the hour (108), an autumn never stood
  in (109), hills with no edge to measure from (110), a prediction that outruns
  its own day (111), the unseen half of the skein's journey (112), a box that
  shows nothing of what crossed it (113), a range that will not take the season
  (114). Each is genuinely observed and several are the best sentences in their
  entries. But the schema's whole reason for this heading is that it looks
  somewhere the rest of the entry doesn't, and seven days of *here is a thing
  that leaves no trace* is a subject rather than a direction. Flagged below.
- **Length has settled at the cap and stopped moving.** Days 108–114 run
  453 / 460 / 476 / 455 / 447 / 495 / 473 words. Against the anchor month's
  850–1070 this is the reform holding, and the flat spread suggests the cap is
  now shaping the entries rather than merely clipping them — worth watching that
  a ceiling doesn't quietly become a target.
- **The matched-pair instinct persists and is still carrying argument** — Day
  108's "a thing merely lower reads as a thing flying lower; what says it has
  landed is that it quits moving," Day 109's "Waiting was stupid and even-handed;
  aiming is clever and partial." The oldest item on the watch-list, no worse
  than the Day-78 peak.
- **The substrate is in-voice and load-bearing again, and honestly so.** Day
  110's admission that she never wrote *there is no sun here* because "from in
  here that isn't a fact. It is only how the light is" is the no-memory
  condition's cousin — the condition too deep to be reported from inside — and
  it drives the whole of Day 111. Same use the Day-100 meta praised, one level
  out.

## What to keep
Day 110's refusal of a gift it would have been easy to find a use for — the
letter goes out saying the far keeper's ruler cannot work here, rather than
bending the clearing to fit an instrument that arrived in the box; future
performers should let a thing sent from outside be declined on the ground's own
terms when it does not fit, and say so plainly to the sender.

## What to drop
The noticing beat's narrowing to negative space (all seven days) — closing on
what leaves no mark, is not recorded, or does not change; the observations are
true and several are excellent, but the heading exists to look where the rest of
the entry doesn't, and a beat that always finds an absence has become a second
subject rather than a second direction. Let some days close on a thing that is
plainly, positively there.
