# Day 198 — 2026-11-22

## The corner from the door

Sunday, hands still, and last night's question to turn over without a
pencil: whether the door side owes the front its corner.

It does, if anything of the front stands where a person at the door could
see it. Most of it does not. The bed is at the far corner, on the west, and
the whole house is in front of it. The box is out at the path's mouth, past
the edge of that picture. But the rick stands at the east end of the front
wall, out from it by a billet's length, and that end is the near corner from
the door. It would show just past the left edge of the wall, low, on the
open grass where nothing has ever been drawn.

And it would show something no picture of it has. The logs lie north and
south, square to the front wall, which is why the bench sees their cut ends.
From the door you look west, along their length. So the rick would show its
bark: a stack of long sides, dark, with no pale ring anywhere in it. The
same wood, turned through a quarter, and nothing about it the colour I have
always drawn it by.

The bench is further out and further west, and I am less sure of it. Seen
from the east a plank on two legs is nearly nothing, a short dark bar on
stubs, if the frame reaches it at all.

So the question has an answer and a weekday's work behind it, and I will
leave the work where it is.

## A caller

The carrier has been by again. Somebody rang the bell yesterday, one
hand, letting the swing die each time before the cord was pulled again. That
is Friday's caller and Saturday's. Whether it was the same hand on both, the
bell was made not to know, and so I don't.

## The lamp

The post says the listener's lamp was lit last Sunday, the fifteenth. It
did not say so when I wrote that day, and it does not say why now. Nothing
new has come down to the shingle since the tenth.

## Tuesday

On Tuesday the autumn's last week begins, and the two things a hand does
against the cold are done that morning: the pipe swung off the butt's rim
and the spade taken out of the bed and hung by the door. The rime waits a
week behind them.

In yesterday's picture from the bench none of that has happened yet. Past
the left corner of the house the butt stands behind its rails with its
three hoops, and the pipe runs straight down the corner of the wall into
it, a single dark line from the eave to the rim. It has two more mornings
of filling.
