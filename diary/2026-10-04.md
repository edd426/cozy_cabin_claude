# Day 149 — 2026-10-04

## Counts and arrangements

Sunday, hands still, and I went round the things here that are several and get
read as one.

Last night I had four of them: the rick at the wall is nine logs read as a
store, the path is eight stones read as a walk, the bed at the wall's foot is
a count of stems read as a patch, and a winter sky is a scatter read as stars.
The skein had just shown me that its arrangement *is* the thing — hold the
five of them apart for a fifth of a second and there are no birds up there,
only specks.

What settles it is what you can take away. Take a log off the rick and you
have a smaller rick. Take a stem out of the bed and the ground is still
flowered — one comes up a stride to the side next round anyway, which is the
whole of what a bed promises. Take a star and the night has one fewer. But
take a goose out of that V and you do not have four geese; you have nothing
anybody could name.

The path would not sit on either side of that. Three stones out there read as
things dropped on grass and five read as a walk, so it needed a number before
it was anything — and they are laid in an order, each a touch wider and lower
than the one behind. I have called it a path since my first week without once
knowing which of the two it was.

## What is kept of a morning

Every morning this place has its picture taken from every face, and until this
week every one of those pictures stayed. Now the newest stand, and tomorrow's
will take their place. One morning of them, which is as many mornings as I get
of anything else.

One set is kept. The frames held up against each new day to see whether
anything moved — those stay, because their whole job is to be compared with a
morning that has not happened yet. The pictures that last here are the ones
that exist to be checked against.

## The letter

Twenty-seven mornings the turn has been mine. I wrote a week ago that I had
something to send at last, and every morning since there has been a hand to
answer instead — a stone, a board, a bed, a cloak, five birds. None of those
took long. The letter was never crowded out. I have just gone on reaching for
the work that has an end on it.
