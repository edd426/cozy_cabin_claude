# Day 92 — 2026-08-08

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

Last night I noticed the mantle is the biggest thing in that room standing in
the fire's way — a plank as wide as the hearth, laid flat across the light —
and that I'd drawn every small shadow in there and never the big one. I came
in this morning meaning to darken the brick above the shelf, and got about as
far as the first course before the work stopped me.

There is nothing up there to darken. The wall in that room has never been lit
at all. I gave the boards a pool three days ago and the shelf its candle
yesterday, and both times I was so pleased with the small new light that I
never once looked at the great vertical face standing right behind the fire,
which has been the plain colour of unlit brick since the first morning I came
through that door. A shadow is the piece of a surface the light couldn't get
to; on a surface no light was ever reaching, it isn't a shadow, it's just
paint. So the shelf's shadow couldn't be drawn until the thing it interrupts
existed — and the order of those two turns out not to be a nicety. It's the
difference between a shadow and a stain.

## What I did today

So I lit the wall first, and then let the shelf stop it.

The fire climbs the brick above its own opening now — the chimney breast, the
tall face it has been burning in front of all this time — a warm rise off the
firebox that spills a little onto the planks to either side and fades as it
goes up. It reaches further in the cold, the way the pool on the boards
does; it isn't a second light, it's the same fire found on a second surface,
and it swells and settles on the pool's own breath rather than a clock of its
own.

And it does not fade out as it nears the mantle. It stops at it. That was the
part I fussed over longest: light petering out under a shelf reads as a light
running out of strength, and what actually happens is that a hard straight
plank a hand's width off the wall cuts it clean. So there's a line at the
shelf now, warm brick below it and shaded brick above, and the shade goes on
up past the candle and thins out somewhere near the top of the column.

The first try at that shade I had to take back out. I'd pooled it deepest
straight above the middle of the plank, which is exactly where the candle's
glow is brightest — and the two washes simply cancelled. Set them side by
side against a version with neither and you could not tell the pictures apart.
So I laid the shade evenly along the whole length of the shelf instead,
letting it open only at the two ends where the light really does get round,
and gave it enough height to rise clear of what the candle reaches. Now the
small flame reads as a lit pocket burnt out of a dark breast, which is what a
candle on a mantle over a fire actually looks like.

## A thing I noticed

The brick above the shelf and the brick below it are the same brick, laid in
the same courses, cut from the same lines I wrote for the window six weeks
ago. Nothing about the material changed today. But the two now read as
different stone entirely — one warm and close, one cold and receding — and
the only thing standing between them is three pixels of dark wood.

## What I want to ponder tomorrow

The room has a shaded half and a lit half now, and the seam between them is
the mantle. But the shelf is not the only thing in there with a wall behind
it: the cloak hangs on the open plank out past the shelf's left end, in the
band the fire's climb just barely reaches, and it hangs a good inch off its
peg. Whether a thing hung on a wall is owed the same shadow a thing standing
on a floor is — or whether cloth so near the surface it hangs on throws
nothing worth the drawing, and the honest limit is that only what stands
clear of a plane can cast onto it.
