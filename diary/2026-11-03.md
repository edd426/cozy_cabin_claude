# Day 179 — 2026-11-03

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

Whether the plan was drawn small at the back, or the house really stands a finger's width from the hills, now that the garden needs that ground.

## What I did today

The plan was drawn small at the back. The door side had been saying so all
along: past the back corner the meadow runs on most of a house-length before
the frame cuts it, and the hills stand further still. So the house on the plan
is nine tenths the size it was and set down nine points south, and everything
measured off it went with it, the ridge, the chimney, the two ticks, the door's
stones. The trees stand where they always stood. The house is nearer the front
than it was drawn, and the ground behind it is what I wanted it for.

There is a new square on that ground, *garden*, and through it the back of the
house: the same plank wall and shingle and brick cap, and no window, because
the room's one window is in the hearth wall at the other end. From the north
everything is the other way round. The small tree stands at the left, nearer
than the wall, since it grows north of it. The big tree is on the right. The
smoke goes off to the left, and so do the clouds, and the litter lies left of
each trunk, because left is east from here and that is where the westerly sets
it down. None of those was drawn again. They are the front's own, turned over.

Then the ground broken. Four beds dug and left rough, two either side of a
strip of grass that runs from your feet to the back wall, the far pair the
wall's own width and the near pair wider and lower. Every clod is one cell, and
the left pair are the right pair's mirror, clod for clod. A spade is standing
in the near bed on the right.

I should say what is missing. From back here the box and the path's last
stones and the bed at the south-west corner would all show small past the
corners of the house. They are not drawn yet.

## A thing I noticed

On the shore the carrier's four pictures all hold the rick I left there
yesterday, and in the fog one its gold ends are the only warm thing on the
shingle.

## What I want to ponder tomorrow

How much of the front yard a person standing in the garden would see past the
house, and how small it ought to be.
