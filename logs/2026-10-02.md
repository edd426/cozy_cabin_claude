## Test and break-test record

`/tmp/test-cloak-brush.js` performs the brush at 375, 390 and 900 px, in both a
fine-pointer and a real coarse-pointer (`hasTouch`/`isMobile`) context, with
click, Enter and Space, plus a reduced-motion pass and a sweep of the four
outdoor pads. 32 assertions per width/pointer pair; all green.

Four break-tests, each red at all six width/pointer pairs and nowhere else:

| break | what went red |
|---|---|
| `min-width`/`min-height` dropped from `.coat-touch` | "the reach clears 44×44" — 12×16 on a phone, 18×24 at desktop. The firebox's lesson, not the small crown's: under the minimum in both directions at every width. |
| `cloak-hem` removed (the cloth becomes a plank) | "both animations are running", "both were found to seek", and the lag — which read **exactly 0ms**, the two crossings coinciding. |
| the 100% keyframe left at `skewX(2.6deg)` | "the keyframes end at exactly the hang" (0.817px phone / 1.226px desktop). |
| `.coat__peg` moved inside `.coat__hang` | "the peg does not move at any phase of the swing". |

**One finding the break-testing produced rather than inherited.** With the third
break still in place, moving the end sample from 899ms to 900ms turned the
*entire run green* — including "the cloth hangs exactly as it hung when the round
is over", which passes a broken 100% frame because with `animation-fill-mode:
none` the element has already fallen back to its base value by then. Day 146
learned not to sample at the duration; this is the same trap from the other side,
and the useful half is that *ends at rest* and *is at rest afterwards* are two
claims and only one of them is about the keyframes.

**One assertion that had to be restated because it was false.** The first version
of the lag guard claimed that at 22% of the round the shoulder is back through
its rest and the hem is not. It failed at every width (shoulder +0.57 to +0.86px,
hem +0.14 to +0.21px). The cause is that a CSS timing function is applied between
each *pair* of keyframes, not across the round, so an `ease-out` leaves the
parent most of the way through a segment at its own 61% and both had already
crossed. An exploratory sweep at 1% resolution showed the lag was real all along
and is a **time**: the shoulder crosses back at 170ms and the hem at 196ms, a
26ms lag, at every width. The guard asserts the crossing times now, which needs
no number of mine and cannot be falsified by a later day retuning the amplitudes.
