## What shipped

**The skein startles.** The eighth thing in the clearing that answers a hand,
and the first whose answer is not one body moving but five bodies losing their
arrangement.

- **The reach is the first pad here that travels.** `.birds-touch` is a *child*
  of `.sprite--birds`, so `bird-cross` carries it across the sky with the
  flock. Exactly `--tap-min` square and centred on the formation's own middle
  (11px across, 9px up in the wrapper's local coordinates); the bird offsets are
  fixed native px at both scales, so it needs no mobile rebase.
- **`aria-hidden` moved off the wrapper onto the five `.bird` cells.** A
  focusable pad inside an `aria-hidden` subtree is a thing shut out of — the
  line Day 144's stone pad drew. Break-tested: putting it back on the wrapper
  reddens the test at all five width/pointer pairs.
- **The scatter sums to zero.** Per-bird `--scatter-x` / `--scatter-y` on
  `.bird--N`, each pointing outward from the flock's centroid: (-4,+1), (-1,-2),
  (+2,-3), (0,+2), (+3,+2). Both axes total 0, so the flock's centre of mass is
  unmoved — the vow written as arithmetic rather than as a sentence. This is the
  day's load-bearing number and the one assertion that nothing else can make:
  the break-test that nudged `bird--3` by one pixel left "every bird goes
  outward" and "every displacement is 2–4px" honestly green.
- **Neither running clock is disturbed.** `bird-cross` is on the wrapper and
  `bird-startle` on the children, so they cannot collide; and each child's
  `bird-flap` stays at index 0 of the longhand `animation-*` lists (Day 146).
  To get a two-item `animation-delay` without five hand-copied numbers, the
  per-bird beat offsets moved from literals in `.bird--N` into a `--beat`
  custom property read by `.bird`.
- **Per-keyframe timing functions**, because a function applies between each
  *pair* of keyframes (Day 147): `ease-out` on the fling (0→18%), `ease-in-out`
  on the knitting back (40→100%). No ring back through rest — a goose is under
  her own power the whole way, the bee's distinction (Day 146).
- **`honk()`, the seventh sound, and `call()`, the third shaper.** The first
  with a *source and a resonator*: a sawtooth through a **peaking** formant
  whose centre sweeps while the call sounds, then a lowpass for the distance.
  Peaking and not bandpass on purpose — a resonator lifts a band, it does not
  delete the rest, and a bandpass takes the fundamental out from under the call.
  Three calls at three pitches, none starting together, plus one short slice of
  the shared noise buffer as the breath under the nearest bird: the first sound
  here that is both noise and tone, because a voice is both. Over in 0.49s.
  `ASSETS.md` has its row.
- **`touch.js`'s dispatch is a table now.** The two listeners said the same
  thing twice — once as early returns, once as a chain of seven nested
  ternaries — and an eighth would have made the second unreadable. One ordered
  `PADS` list read by both. Semantics are identical (pads never nest, so at most
  one selector can match a target); the test exercises all eight across three
  views to prove nothing was dropped.

## The finding: where a hand cannot reach them

A positioned `z-index` opens a stacking context, so nothing inside
`.sprite--birds` (z-index 3) can be lifted above anything outside it — the pad
sits in the flock's plane whatever number it is given. The map card is pinned
over the top-right corner at z-index 6. So for the stretch of the crossing that
passes behind that card, **the card takes the press and the skein cannot be
startled at all**. Day 137 noticed the birds fly close enough to clip the card's
edge; this is what that costs now the sky can be touched. The test asserts the
card wins there rather than asserting no overlap, because the overlap is honest:
the geese are behind it.

### One thing the testing taught

`locator.boundingBox()` disagreed with `getBoundingClientRect()` at 375px, and
then the rect itself came back **43.999996185302734** where the computed style
said `44px`. It is a float32's nearest neighbour to 44, arrived at because the
crossing holds the flock at a fractional offset and the frame's own `8%`
baseline is fractional too. Same class as reading `AudioParam.value` back and
finding `0.10999999940395355` where `sound.js` declares `0.11` (Day 145): a
measured number is not the number that was written, and a `>=` against an exact
integer is a guard that fails for arithmetic rather than for the yard. The
tolerance is `1e-4` and the reason is written beside it. The byproduct is a
better assertion than the one I set out to write — the test now measures both
the pad's own size *and* how much of it survives the frame's `overflow: hidden`,
which is what a finger can actually land on.
