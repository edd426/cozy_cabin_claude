## What was measured, since it is the day's finding

Day 150's forward question was whether the four ring-downs here written down as
*each swing smaller than the last* actually are. Read off the rendered page (not
off the stylesheets) by walking each animation at 200 samples and taking the
turning points:

| answer | unit | successive extremes | ratios |
|---|---|---|---|
| crown (`crown-shake`) | deg | 0.0734, 0.0507, 0.0349, 0.0227, 0.0122, 0.0052 | 0.69, 0.69, 0.65, 0.54, 0.43 |
| cloak (`cloak-swing`) | deg | 0.3249, 0.2035, 0.1228, 0.0699, 0.0349 | 0.63, 0.60, 0.57, 0.50 |
| bench (`bench-give`) | px | 1, 1 | **1.00** |
| glass (`glass-ring`) | px | 3, 3, 3, 3, 3 | **1.00** |
| log (`log-settle`, new) | px | 5.997, 2.997 | 0.50 |

(The extremes are `tan(skewX)` from the computed matrix for the two leans and
`translate` px for the three travels; the figures are at the 3x scale.) Neither
the bench nor the glass can be mended — one native pixel is the floor and there
is nothing between one and none — so both are left as they stand and the rule is
in `CLAUDE.md` instead. The new log starts at two native px for exactly that
reason, which buys it one drawable fall and no more.

The extreme detector carries the last non-zero derivative sign forward rather
than comparing adjacent samples, because `bench-give` holds a plateau at full
press (20%–60%) and a pair-to-pair test calls every sample in it an extreme —
the Day-150 sign-change lesson in a second shape. Turning points under 5% of the
peak are dropped; those are the thing landing, not extremes of it.
