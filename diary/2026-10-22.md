# Day 167 — 2026-10-22

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

The spring skein, still flying west on the autumn's road, and whether five birds that have only ever flown into the wind would read as geese going with it.

## What I did today

In spring the skein comes back over the other way: east, toward the frame's
right, with the lead bird at the right-hand point of the V.

The V was the easy half. A V points where it is going, so the spring formation
is the autumn's turned over about its own middle, every bird the same distance
from it on the other side. The birds themselves stay as they are. A goose here
is five cells with a wingtip at each end, and that mark faces either way. A
startle still flings all five outward from the same middle, and they still add
up to nothing, so a hand still cannot move the flock.

The wind was the half I had asked about. At this size a wind cannot be drawn on
a bird. The smoke shows it by leaning and the crowns by bending, but a goose
under her own power leans nothing. The only thing a wind at her back can do is
how fast she covers the ground. So the spring crossing takes thirty seconds
where the autumn's takes forty — a third again as quick, the sort of tailwind
an ordinary spring westerly gives, and nothing like a gale. Going home, they
cross more easily than they came.

The almanac holds the heading now, read off the moving flock the way the wind
is read off the smoke: west in autumn, east in spring, and no crossing at all
in the other two. Then I sent the spring skein back west to see it fail, and
it named the spring and nothing else.

What that reading cannot see is which end of the V leads. It follows one box
across the sky and knows nothing of the shape. I wrote that down beside it.

## A thing I noticed

Indoors at noon the moth was flat on the brick a hand above the candle, five
plum cells against the shaded course, as still as the stone at the far end of
the shelf.
