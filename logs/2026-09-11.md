## Why tiers and not sizes

The load-bearing decision of the day, and the reason the diff is small. The
flame is three absolutely-positioned rectangles and the **season** already sets
the geometry of each (summer 10w×3h → 6w → 3w; winter 10w×4h → 8w → 4w×2h). A
multiplicative hour scalar on those sizes would have done two bad things:

1. **Fractional widths on a pixel sprite.** 6 native × 0.85 = 5.1, which at
   `--s: 3` is a 15.3px box — a soft edge that `image-rendering: pixelated`
   cannot mend. Everything in this project is on the native grid (Day 120's
   "a broken grid reads as *wrong*, not as *near*", one level in).
2. **A 4 × 4 combinatorial table.** Writing season × hour geometry out is 16
   rules per layer, and the two tables would drift apart the first time one of
   them was edited.

Gating whole tiers with `display` avoids both: the hour says only *how many*
stand, so each season keeps its own fire and all four bank identically out of
one block. It is also what makes the claim countable — `visible-count` reads
`getClientRects()`, which is immune to the three tiers' opacity flicker (the
Day-98 rule: count on layout, not brightness), and `display: none` is honestly
absent to it.

|  | base | flame | tip | tiers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| day / dusk (identity, no rule) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 3 |
| dawn | ✓ | ✓ | — | 2 |
| night (banked) | ✓ | — | — | 1 |

The ember bed answers the other way about — 1 native row of the 2-native bed lit
at the hours the flame stands, the whole bed at dawn and through the night,
because that is where the fire went. Fewest tiers, most coal.

## Strength, not reach

`--firelight` is declared on `.scene--inside` and inherits (custom properties
do) to `.hearth-light`, `.chair` and `.woodpile`, which live outside `.hearth`.
It multiplies **opacity only** on all four lit/shaded surfaces and touches no
size:

- the year says how big this fire is → it sets the light's **reach** (the
  Day-89 `--light-w`/`--light-h` table, the Day-92 `--wl-w`/`--wl-h` table, the
  Day-90 `--cast` lengths — all untouched and still the season's);
- the hour says how hard it is burning → it sets only the light's **strength**.

That division is what keeps Day 90's rule intact without a cascade: a shadow can
be no darker than the light it interrupts, but nothing on that floor moves at
midnight. Measured at 375 (`--s: 2`), summer:

| band | tiers | `.hearth-light` opacity | `.chair::before` opacity | ember inset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dawn | 2 | 0.605 | 0.630 | 4px (2 native) |
| day | 3 | 0.720 | 0.750 | 2px (1 native) |
| dusk | 3 | 0.720 | 0.750 | 2px |
| night | 1 | 0.418 | 0.435 | 4px |

## The repointed vow check

The `kept` vow's hour check (`floor: 1` over all four bands, "the hearth lit at
every hour") read **`fire-flame`** — the *middle* tier. The night is now allowed
to put that tier down, on purpose, so left alone the check would have gone red
on a fire doing exactly what it should. It reads `fire-tiers` now. The reasoning
is Day 124's: *a floor is the right shape for a bottom*, and the bottom here is
how many tiers are standing, not whether any particular one of them is. The
flame may be banked to one; it may never be banked to none.

`fire-flame` keeps its two season checks at `tod: 'day'`, both unaffected.
