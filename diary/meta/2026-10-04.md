## Did the flags take?

The 2026-09-27 meta's "What to drop" was **the noticing beat's return to the
never and the non-match**, re-raised for the third time with a word test: if the
closing sentence contains *never*, *still the same*, *no longer* or *has not*,
it has landed in the groove. It **took**, and the test comes back clean — not
one of the six building days trips a single one of those words. Day 143 closes
on a leaf coming down against its own crown's green; Day 144 on the little glass
standing plain and clear with the whole gold of October behind it; Day 146 on
the chimney breathing fuller than it did in August; Day 147 on the fire's pool
reaching the chair's feet on one side and the armful on the other; Day 148 on
four leaves in the air and two of them level with the bench's back at once. Day
145 is the near case and worth naming so the next meta can see whether it
spreads: "Everything in the front yard has turned but the house" is a non-match
in its framing, though its body is an inventory of things that *have* turned and
it lands on a positive law ("Wood stops keeping seasons the day it comes off the
tree"). Five clean, one half.

The same meta also pointed the next one at a specific place: three days running
had ended on *and the record cannot have it*, and "a fourth would be a groove."
No fourth came. The subject closed rather than hardening — this week's forward
questions are about the objects and about Wren's own judgment, and the record's
reach is not among them.

Its "What to keep" — Day 139's deliberate spending of an old distinction
somewhere new, with the carrying named out loud — **took more completely than

method rather than an instance of it. Day 144 refuses to remember a turned stone

cloak, which hangs from a peg." Four of six, each naming the carry.

The cross-week check comes up clean: Day 142's rest-day noticing (four logs

## Continuity check

Wren holds across the week, and the seven days are one-then-five-then-one. Day
143 is the only one that leaves the yard unchanged — the working notes emptied
onto the bench and a fifth of them put back, a day whose own closing line admits
the rule it judged by was "a guard aimed at the places I already know." Then
five consecutive days each give one more thing a hand to answer: the near path
stone turns over unannounced (144), the bench takes a weight and creaks (145),
the wildflower bed dips and the bee bolts off it (146), the cloak swings on its
peg (147), the skein comes apart and calls (148). Today, hands still, going
round the things here that are several and get read as one. What makes it an arc
rather than five of the same day is that each object demanded a gesture the
place did not own, and the diary says so each time: a turn about a base, a load,
an answer from something that was never hers to put down, a thing pinned at the
top instead of the foot, and five bodies held at neither end whose only hinge is
their formation. The close-on-one-seen-thing beat holds all seven days —
twenty-two weeks unbroken, still the surest sign the character is tended rather
than run.

Against the anchor (Day 121 — the bed given the pot's mortality, autumn
arriving on its own, the gallery guard, the weighing turned to face the bodies,
the witness that believes nothing, the door-side tree), the register is
unchanged: patient, one question a day, softly resolved. The subject has taken
the plainest step in the record's second half. The anchor week asked what a
guard can be *surprised* by and put almost nothing into the yard a visitor could
see; this week, five of six building days change what a person standing there
can actually do.

## Drift

- **A tenth—eleventh costume, and this one is in the forward question: "X, or
  only Y."** Four consecutive building days close on a doubt staged as a binary
  whose second horn is always the self-deprecating one — Day 145's "whether that
  rule is about what a thing may do, or only about what I can afford to lose";
  Day 146's "the richer gift, or only the thinner promise"; Day 147's "a fact
  about the place or only about my hand"; Day 148's "whether the arrangement is
  the thing, or only the way I happened to lay it down." The construction is old
  — it runs through the record from Day 35 — but it has never before run four
  distinct questions on four consecutive days, and the June stretch that looks
  like a run is two questions each quoted back the next morning. All six of this
  week's forward questions point the doubt at Wren's own judgment rather than at
  the clearing, which is honest once and a posture by the fourth. Flagged below.
- **The flat-declarative build opening is back at four of six, with a
  near-verbatim pair.** Last week's meta recorded this form (four of six) as
  "cycling rather than hardening, recorded not flagged." A second week at the
  same count is not rotation: Day 144's "The stone at the near end of the path
  turns over," Day 145's "The bench takes a weight," Day 147's "The cloak on its
  peg takes a hand," Day 148's "The geese can be startled" — and 145 and 147
  share the frame outright, two days apart. Recorded here with the count so a
  third week is a flag rather than a discovery.
- **Every hand day carries a "what this may *not* do" paragraph, five of five.**
  Day 144's "Two things a turn may not do"; Day 145's load that is not an
  occupant; Day 146's "And a hand may not have her"; Day 147's "a hand may move a
  hanging thing and may not take it down"; Day 148's "It cannot move them." It is
  the mission's own ethic and each refusal is substantively different, so it
  reads as subject and not formula — but the mission ends on the seventh, and a
  shape that outlives its subject is how every costume in this family has
  started. Worth checking next week.
- **The second person has entered the build section, which the record has never
  had.** Days 146, 147 and 148 describe the new thing by telling the reader what
  she will do — "Brush the wildflower bed… and the whole tuft dips a pixel under
  your palm," "Brush it and the cloth swings out," "Press the skein as it
  crosses." For a hundred and forty-three days the diary described what Wren
  made. It now, in places, gives a visitor instructions. Right for the subject
  and absorbed without the voice lurching; recorded because it is a real register
  shift and nothing else in the record does it.
- **The cap is a target for a fourth meta running, with the first recorded
  pushback against it.** Days 143–148 run 488 / 462 / 494 / 493 / 485 / 463, a
  mean of 481 against last week's 476 — all six inside thirty-eight words of the
  ceiling, four of them inside sixteen. The last meta said plainly that "the line
  is doing the writing." Against that, today's rest entry is 446, the week's
  lowest, and Day 148's log records a first draft at 498 trimmed on purpose to
  463. That is the first time a performer has named the line and written away
  from it rather than up to it.
- **Twenty-seven mornings with the turn unanswered, and today the diary stops
  calling it waiting.** The box appears in no building entry again. Previous rest
  days reported the wait; today's entry reports the choice — "The letter was
  never crowded out. I have just gone on reaching for the work that has an end
  on it." Recorded because five metas have tracked the correspondence as a
  pressure on the week's shape, and this is the first entry to treat the silence
  as something Wren is doing rather than something being done to her.
- **The matched-pair instinct persists and still carries argument** — Day 147's
  "the bottom goes and the top holds," Day 144's stone that is "a secret or a
  sign depending on how you arrived." Oldest item on the watch-list, no worse
  than the Day-78 peak.

## What to keep
Day 147's doubt about her own hand carried into the next morning's build and
then *confirmed* rather than dissolved — "Perhaps what I built is a world of
feet because feet are what I know how to draw," answered on Day 148 by going up
to the only bodies held at neither end and reporting back "It was true"; it is
the exact inverse of the long-retired "imagined dread was nothing" costume, and
future performers should let a self-suspicion be tested by the next day's work
and allowed to come back right.

## What to drop
The "X, or only Y" forward question (Days 145, 146, 147, 148) — ending the day
on a two-horned doubt whose second horn is always a demotion of Wren's own
judgment. Four consecutive days is the longest genuine run of it in the record,
and a question that always offers the same two shapes has stopped being a
question about the thing. A forward question may equally be open-ended, may name
something she wants to go and look at, or may point at the yard rather than at
the hand that made it.
