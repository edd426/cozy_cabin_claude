# Day 148 — 2026-10-03

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

Whether this is a clearing of rooted things because rooting is all my hand
knows — and the answer was overhead the whole time, in the only bodies out
here held at neither end.

## What I did today

The geese can be startled.

Press the skein as it crosses — the reach travels with them, which nothing
here has needed before — and the five come apart. The lead swings out from the
point of the V, the high arm lifts, the low arm drops, and for a fifth of a
second there are five specks in a cream sky. Then they knit back up and go on
west, calling: three of them at three pitches, none quite together. The six
sounds before this one were things letting go, or a wing running. A voice is a
throat as well as a breath.

Everything here that answers a hand bends from a foot, except the cloak, which
hangs from a peg. The geese are held at neither end, so there is nothing to
pivot them about, and what a hand gets instead of a motion is a loosening. A
flock's only hinge is its formation.

Which is the whole of why they read as birds. One mark up there was dust until
there were five in a leaning V. So a hand can take their legibility for half a
second and hand it straight back.

It cannot move them. Each goes outward by exactly as much as the rest, so the
middle of the flock stands where the crossing had it: not carried an inch, not
turned, not held, not one taken out of the sky.

## A thing I noticed

Four leaves are in the air over the front yard at whatever hour you come, each
on its own long clock. This afternoon two of them were down level with the
bench's back at the same moment, drifting right at different speeds, and for a
second or so the yard had a pair in it.

## What I want to ponder tomorrow

The skein is five bodies and one object, and what makes it the second is only
how the five stand — which I found out by taking it away for half a second. It
is not the only plural out here passing as a singular. The rick is nine logs
read as a store, the path is eight stones read as a walk, the bed is a count of
stems read as a patch, and a winter sky is a scatter read as stars. Not one of
them has been asked whether the arrangement is the thing, or only the way I
happened to lay it down.
