## The one mechanism worth carrying forward

`.sprite--bee` was already running `bee-forage` on `transform`, and Day 140's
lesson is that the `animation` **shorthand** resets `animation-name` and would
stop that round dead. The workaround used today, and it works:

```css
.sprite--bee.is-startled {
  animation-name: bee-forage, bee-startle;
  animation-duration: 30s, 0.80s;
  animation-timing-function: ease-in-out, ease-out;
  animation-iteration-count: infinite, 1;
}
```

Adding a second entry to the **longhand** lists, with the running animation
left at index 0 under its own name, does **not** restart it — Chromium matches
CSS animations by (name, index). The test asserts this directly rather than
assuming it: it reads `bee-forage`'s `currentTime` before the press (~2000ms)
and 140ms after, and requires the second to be the larger. Break-testing with
the shorthand in place reddens that line immediately (`2000 -> 0ms`) and drops
`bee-forage` off `getAnimations()` entirely.

The second half of the trick is that `bee-startle` animates the **`translate`**
property rather than `transform`. Individual transform properties compose with
`transform` rather than replacing it, so the bolt rides on top of the forage
round without either one knowing about the other. Day 140's rule — a one-shot
on an already-animating element must pick a property the running animation does
not own — now has a second instance, and `translate` / `rotate` / `scale` are
the obvious free properties anywhere `transform` is taken.

`/tmp/test-bed-brush.js` (disposable, not committed) — 113 assertions. Performs
the brush at 375, 390 and 900 by click, Enter, Space and a real coarse-pointer
`tap()`, plus a `reducedMotion: 'reduce'` pass. It asserts:

- the reach clears 44×44 at every width, spans the whole bed (measured off the
  rendered blossom rects, not off the CSS), and contains the bee's rest rect;
- it overlaps none of `.crown-touch--left`, `.crown-touch--right`,
  `.bench-touch`, `.stone-touch--front`, `.sprite--mailbox`, `.map-button`;
- it carries `cursor: pointer`, the button role, tabindex and a label, and is
  not inside anything `aria-hidden`;
- `bee-forage` is well into its round before the press and **further into it**
  after (the mechanism above);
- both animations stand on the bee and `bloom-stir` runs on **every** stem the
  bed holds today, not only the hand-sown three (6/6 this morning — the count
  moves with the date, `garden.js` Day 115);
- she goes exactly 7px up, stops dead at the top, and ends exactly at her
  round's own position;
- the stems dip exactly 1px and return;
- no audio context exists before any press, exactly one after, exactly **two
  oscillators** and **zero buffer sources** started, and the master gain is the
  declared `PEAK` read back off the live page (compared with a tolerance —
  `AudioParam.value` is a float32, Day 145);
- a second brush mid-bolt is ignored rather than holding her in the air, and
  the bed is not re-dipped under it;
- both classes come off at `animationend` and the bee is left carrying no
  displacement of her own;
- under reduced motion the buzz arrives in full, both classes are already off,
  and nothing is seen to move;
- the Day-139 crown, the Day-144 stone, the Day-145 bench and the map card all
  still answer.

Break-tested three ways, each red where it should be and nowhere else:

1. the reach's `min-width`/`min-height` and explicit box dropped to 36×20 →
   red at all three widths, on the 44×44 line, the bed-coverage line and the
   bee-inside-the-reach line;
2. `.sprite--bee.is-startled` written with the `animation` **shorthand** (the
   Day-140 trap) → red at once on `her round goes on underneath the bolt
   (2000 -> 0ms)` and on `both animations on the bee`;
3. `bee-startle`'s 100% keyframe changed from `0` to `-2px`, so she ends two
   pixels above where her round left her → red on exactly one line.

Break 3 is the one worth writing down, because the **first** time it was run it
came back green. The assertion was sampling the bolt at `currentTime = 800`,
which is the round's full duration — and with `animation-fill-mode: none` the
element has already fallen back to its base value at that point, so a 100%
keyframe that did not return to rest read as resting anyway. The guard was
green and had never once been able to fail. Sampling at **799** puts the
reading inside the active interval, and the same break reddens it at all three
widths. Day 141's lesson from a third side: *a guard asked only where it has
nothing to do will always look like a guard that does nothing* — and here the
place it had nothing to do was not an hour or a season but one millisecond past
the end of the thing it was watching.

(A second, smaller test-hygiene note: seeking paused animations leaves them
parked short of their end, so `animationend` never arrives and the class never
comes off. Four later assertions were reading a page the test itself had
broken. The measurement block now `play()`s everything back before the next
step.)
