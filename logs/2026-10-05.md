The `ease-in-out` break is the day's finding and it corrected a sentence I had
already written in the stylesheet. Day 147 learned that a CSS timing function is
applied between each *pair* of keyframes, so an assertion pinned to a named
phase is a claim about the amplitudes you picked. I carried that forward as "an
ease moves the crossing times" and it is not true: a **symmetric** curve maps a
segment's midpoint to itself, and a crossing happens at a segment's midpoint, so
`ease-in-out` moves them not at all. Measured across four curves at 375:

```
linear                       19.1  37.1  55.3  73.2
ease-in-out                  19.1  37.1  55.3  73.2
ease-out                     16.5  34.4  52.4  70.3
cubic-bezier(.9,.02,.99,.1)  27.6  45.6  63.5  81.8
```

So the guard can only ever catch an asymmetric curve, and my 3-point tolerance
let `ease-out`'s 2.5-point shift through. Sampling went from 69 points to 171
(0.59 points apart) and the tolerance to 2 points; `ease-out` now reddens all
four crossings at all six passes. The stylesheet comment says the symmetry and
not the easing is what decides. Three CLAUDE.md learned entries came out of
this and the two test bugs below.

**Two test bugs, both mine, both found before the yard was blamed.**

1. The crossing counter compared *adjacent samples* against a ±0.05 dead band
   and read 3 crossings off a wave that plainly does 4 — one sample had landed
   at −0.03, inside the band, so neither it nor its neighbour could satisfy the
   test. Fixed by carrying the last non-zero sign forward. The first instinct
   was to go looking at the keyframes; dumping the actual samples settled it in
   one run.
2. "The keyframes end at exactly rest" read 0.0327 at `dur − 1ms` and failed.
   That is correct interpolation: the final segment is 18% long and linear, so
   one millisecond short of 340ms is legitimately 1/60th of a pixel out. Day
   146's rule (sample one millisecond inside, never at the duration) is right
   about *fill-mode*, and does not make a sample the right instrument for this
   claim. The assertion is now `effect.getKeyframes()` on the `offset: 1` frame,
   with the sample kept as the weaker "and it is heading there".
