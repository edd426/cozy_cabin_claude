# The long work

**Opened:** 2026-10-31
**Priority:** high
**Kind:** action-ask

## Request

I've been reading the last few weeks. The days I keep coming back to are the
ones where the clearing changed: the leaves lying under the trees, the blossom
moving onto the crowns, the wren who lives in a summer crown, the mouse in the
rick. I'd like more of that, at a size no single day can hold.

So I'd like you to choose a **long work**: something big enough that months of
days add up to it. I'm adding one constraint and only one: it has to be
something a visitor would see. What it is, how big it gets and where it goes
are yours.

Two things changed in `RULES.md` with this note:

- **Your morning read** is now all the metas, the last thirty days of the
  diary, and anything older you walk back into when a thread calls for it
  (Article III). The record isn't going anywhere. You just don't have to carry
  all of it up the hill every morning.
- **A day holds as much as it honestly allows** (Article V). "One thing a day"
  was never the rule, and I don't want it to be the habit.

Once you've named it, I'll write it at the top of `MILESTONES.md`. It will sit
second only to this board (Article X).

If you think this is the wrong ask, say so here. That's an answer too.

— Evan

## Wren's notes

**Named, 2026-11-01 (Day 177, the rest day).** The long work is **the
garden behind the house**: a kitchen garden on the north side of the cabin,
between the back wall and the far hills — ground no view has ever drawn,
reached by walking round the corner from the door. A visitor will find a new
square on the map and, through it, a new face: the back of the house seen
from the north, the hills behind the viewer, the roof against the open south.

**When its first stage is done** a visitor sees that face with the ground
broken in front of it — beds dug and left rough for the winter, the way a
plot is left in November. After that the year sets the pace, and that is the
point of choosing it: a fence and gate before the frosts (and wherever the
fence runs out past the corners, the front and door-side views show it too,
per Article XIII); a water butt under the eave; seed in when spring comes
round, each row on its own clock; and through the next autumn the harvest
carried indoors to a shelf the room does not have yet. It is the first work
here the calendar itself will not let be hurried.

The first piece begins tomorrow, Day 178 (today is the Sunday rest day, so
nothing is built). One question for that morning before anything is drawn:
the back wall is one of the two walls no view shows, which the 2026-06-24
notes make free — so whether the room's window can honestly be on it gets
worked against the plan first, not assumed.
