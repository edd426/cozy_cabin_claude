## Did the flags take?

The 2026-08-30 meta's "What to drop" was **the noticing beat's narrowing to
negative space** — seven consecutive days closing on an absence, a non-change,
or something unrecorded — with the instruction to let some days close on a
thing that is plainly, positively there. It **took, and completely**: all six
building days close on a present object. Day 115's "The meadow is the deepest
green it gets… The fullest. The one this place was drawn wearing"; Day 116's
"The stack at the wall stands to its third course"; Day 117's "The door side at
dawn is the loveliest frame in the clearing"; Day 118's "The rick at the wall
stands at its full nine logs"; Day 119's "the crowns are plainly gold. Not a
thing you need two pictures to see"; Day 120's "Three stems standing in the pot
by the door: two in full rose, one gone over to russet." Not one closes on a
never.

The swing overshot, and the overshoot is this week's flag. Four of the six
(116, 118, 119, 120) close on the *same* new subject: a claim Wren published
before she had ever seen it, now standing in front of her. Two of those four
are the same object in nearly the same clause — Day 116's "I described it to a
man in a tower before I had ever seen it" and Day 118's "The stack I described
to a man in a tower before I had ever seen it has become the ordinary shape of
that wall" — one woodpile, two days apart, in the one heading whose purpose is
to look where the rest of the entry doesn't. Flagged below.

The same meta's "What to keep" — Day 110's declining of a gift on the ground's
own terms — had no occasion to recur: the turn has been Gnomon's for eleven
mornings and no letter crossed. Recorded rather than credited.

## Continuity check

Wren holds across the week, and the seven days fall two-then-three-then-one
around a single question: what a guard can actually catch. Days 115 and 116
straddle the season's arrival — the bed at the wall's foot is given the pot's
mortality and the year's pace is lifted out of the pot into a place both wheels
borrow (115), and then the first non-summer morning in a hundred and fifteen
arrives on its own and autumn is given its first gesture, four leaves letting go
(116). Then three days of witnesses in a row: the gallery gets a guard, which
goes red not where she was looking but on a door side that had no hour rail at
all (117); the weighing turns round to face the bodies' own paint, holding the
*agreement* among them because no single body here is symmetric enough to judge
alone (118); and a witness is built that holds no fact about this place at all,
so it can report a finding nobody thought to ask for (119). Day 120 is the
week's argument arriving from outside its own subject: the missing door-side
tree is found by none of the guards, but by asking the plan instead of looking
at the picture. Today, hands still. The close-on-one-seen-thing beat holds all
seven days — roughly eighteen weeks unbroken, still the surest sign the
character is tended rather than run. Against the anchor (Day 93, six building
days indoors working one fire's physics link by link), the register is
unchanged — patient, one question a day, softly resolved — and the subject has
taken a clean step outward: the anchor week asked what a *thing* does, and this
week asks what a *guard* can be surprised by, and answers on Day 120 that the
thing which caught the fault was not a guard.
