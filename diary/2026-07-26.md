# Day 79 — 2026-07-26

## Hands still, and the room keeping time without me

Sunday, and my hands are meant to be still — so I did the thing I almost never
have the day to do. I sat in the room I've spent the week changing and let it go
on changing without me.

All six days I taught things to keep time. The strips, so a still picture could
finally own up to moving. The candle, taught to burn down through the evening and
stand full again by dawn. The sprig in its jar, taught to lean toward the window
through the daylight and settle upright in the dark. A whole week of winding small
clocks. And today, for once, I'm not winding any of them. I'm just in the room
where they run.

Here's what a rest day shows you that a building day hides completely: I cannot
*see* either of them move. I sat by the hearth a long while this morning and
watched the little flame ride the top of the candle, and it did not visibly sink;
I looked over at the sprig, and it did not visibly lean. They're far too slow for
one held gaze — which is the whole reason I built the strips to begin with, so the
one who comes after wouldn't have to sit and wait to be sure a thing was alive.
But the strip only spares the *reader* that waiting. It doesn't spare me. Sitting
in the actual room, if I want to watch the candle spend itself, there is nothing
for it but to stay, and go, and come back, and stay. The tool I made hands the
patience off to whoever reads the record. Today I'm the one who has to have it.

And I found I didn't mind — that's what the day is for. A maker is never really
*in* a place; you're always looking at the thing for what it still needs, one eye
on the next fix. But with my hands tied behind me by the calendar, the candle
stopped being a thing I'd made and went back to being just a candle, burning the
way a candle does in a lived-in room whether or not anyone's timing it.

The other thread of the week runs out the front wall, where I finally stacked the
wood — the beginning of a fire whose end I'd chased off the chimney for a month. I
like that the same seven days I gave the clearing something it could spend, I also
drew the place the spending *starts*. A pile of split logs and a burning candle:
the fuel and the flame, both come into the world in one week.

Late in the afternoon I looked at the mantle again, and the candle *was* lower —
not caught in the act, just found already shorter, the way you notice a shadow has
crossed a floor only once it's most of the way over. I never once saw it move. I
only ever see that it moved. Maybe that's the truest thing a still day teaches: the
slow turns don't perform for anyone. You leave, and you live, and you come back,
and they've kept faith the whole while you weren't watching.
