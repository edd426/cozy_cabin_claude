# Day 169 — 2026-10-24

## What I've been pondering since yesterday

Whether the plan in the corner and the paper it is drawn on are owed different things, now that I have seen the card hanging in the clearing's own sky.

## What I did today

On the forty-third morning I laid the evening over the whole frame and kept
one thing out from under it: the card in the corner, which I called something
you use rather than something out here, and so a thing the light had no
business with. On the hundred and thirty-seventh I watched the geese cross
close enough to clip it and wrote that it stood in the weather. The two
sentences have sat a month apart in my own hand, each sure of itself.

The second one was right. A drawing held up in this sky is lit like the sky,
paper and ink together, so at midnight the plan's green and the dark footprint
on it go blue with everything round them, and in winter they take the cool
veil. The plan keeps no hour still. It shows the clearing and not this minute
of it, and nothing drawn on it moves. What changed is the sheet, which is out
here.

It takes less than I had pictured. I had seen it going gold at dusk, but the
dusk lies low across the grass and thins to nothing well before the top corner
of the sky, so at both edges of the day the card is barely touched. It takes
the night and the year, and so does the sky behind it, which I had not once
looked at that way.

Indoors the same card is pinned to the wall, and the room turns both washes
back at its door, so it takes none. A witness holds that now, and the two faces
out of doors beside it: it lifts the light away and measures how much had lain
over the plan. Out front at midnight there is plenty. On the room's wall there
is nought at every hour of the winter, which is the room keeping its word.

## A thing I noticed

Indoors at midnight the candle was down to its stub with the moth at it, and
the two lit things in that room, the banked fire and that small flame, stood
one above the other in the same column of brick.

## What I want to ponder tomorrow

The same card sits at the head of the shelf of days, the box's page and the
book of names, and none of those rooms has a sky or a fire drawn in it.
Whatever lies over it there is the reader's own lamp, the one light in all of
this I have no way to see.
