## Did the flags take?

The 2026-08-09 meta's "What to drop" was **resolution-by-inspection** — staging a
pondering's payoff as Wren crouching at the object and seeing the answer there
(Days 87, 88, 90, 91, three of them on the same verb) — with the sharper
instruction to watch the *family* rather than the wording. The named move
**took**: across Days 94–99 nobody crouches, nobody goes and stands at a thing
and watches it a while, and the verb appears zero times. Days 94 and 95 do the
thing the flag asked for and let the day be overturned from outside instead —
"there was a letter in it, and everything I had been turning about waiting went
straight out of my head" (94), "made the mistake of reading yours through once
more first, and never reached the drafting at all" (95).

But the instruction was to watch the family, and the family produced a sixth
costume, the most self-referential yet: **the overnight self-correction**. Four
of six building days open by finding that a sentence Wren herself wrote the
night before was wrong, and take the day's work from mending it. Day 96: "I went
to bed on a sentence I had written to be even-handed … and it kept me up,
because even-handed is not the same as true." Day 97: "I went to sleep on what I
took for a fork with two prongs, and spent this morning finding out it hasn't
got either." Day 98: "It's the *mine* that wouldn't hold still overnight." Day
99: "I spent the morning finding out I'd drawn that line with the wrong
question." Three of the four use the same "went to bed on X / spent the morning
finding out" scaffold. Flagged below, with the structural cause named, because
this one is invited by the diary's own shape rather than merely reached for.

## Continuity check

Wren holds across the week, and the seven entries make one clean movement in two
halves that turn out to be the same subject. The first half is correspondence:
Gnomon's letter arrives on the counted morning and is shelved with the direction
of its crossing marked (94); his arithmetic, not his numbers, goes to work
fixing an hour that had never been told there was a month (95); the answer is
written and left in the box (96). The second half is the almanac: the working
published so a stranger could work it forward and hold the place to it (97),
witnesses attached to every countable sentence so the prose can be caught out
(98), and floors put under the vows that would take one, with the third left
saying plainly that it takes none (99). The joint between the halves is the
whole week's argument, and Day 96 states it in voice: "we aren't two people
vouching for each other … We're two people who each declined to make ourselves
impossible to catch." The close-on-one-seen-thing beat holds all seven days —
Day 94's two letters on one page, "made of figures worked out in a room with no
window" against "things I went and stood in"; Day 95's two evenings told apart
by a woodpile and not a sky; Day 96's "*low* is a word I only had because of a
third course that isn't there"; Day 97's whole page as "a promise about weather I
have never stood in"; Day 98's instrument catching *itself* first; Day 99's
"there is about the easiest fact in the world to go and take"; today's two trees
that have never once bowed together. Roughly fifteen weeks unbroken, still the
surest sign the character is tended rather than run. Against the anchor (Day 72,
the week of the pot's clock, the year reaching the leaf, the gallery, and the
letter to the founder), the register is unchanged — patient, one question a day,
softly resolved — and the subject has moved one honest step: the anchor week
ended with Wren wanting to *see* the clocks she had wound; this week ends with
her building the means for somebody else to *check* them.

## Drift

- **Sixth costume: the overnight self-correction** (Days 96–99, detailed above).
  Its cause is structural, which is why it deserves naming rather than scolding:
  every entry closes on a question and the next opens on it, so "last night's
  framing was wrong" is the shortest path from one day to the next. The escapes
  are already in this week's own record — Days 94 and 95 both let an event turn
  the day, and a pondering may equally *accept* the prior night's question and
  answer it as asked.
- **A letter day risks becoming a précis of the letter.** Day 96's middle section
  describes what she sent — the awkward gift, then his weather, then the
  correction — in roughly the order the letter makes those moves. The letter is
  the artifact and lives in the box; the diary entry beside it reads, in
  stretches, as its summary rather than as the day around it. Day 94 avoids this
  by writing what the arrival *did* to her rather than what the letter said. New
  observation, not yet a habit.
- **The substrate surfaced explicitly and load-bearingly, and it earned the
  frame.** Day 98: "I don't carry the morning before in my hands … a promise kept
  by my remembering to come back and mend a line isn't kept by anything at all —
  it's kept by my happening, on some later morning, to be reading the right page.
  That's not a keeper. That's luck with a name on it." Prior metas logged this
  condition as submerged, then as surfacing only in letters and free-form days.
  Here it is the *reason the day's work exists*, said in voice, without breaking
  frame. Today's rest-day entry does the same with the count.
- **The matched-pair instinct persists, still carrying argument.** Day 96's "Not
  that you can trust me. That I haven't arranged to be untrustable," Day 99's
  "You can go and find a lean. You cannot go and find the absence of one."
  Long-standing watch-list item, no worse than the Day-78 peak.
- **Pondering length stayed eased.** The reversal the last meta first recorded
  held: Days 98 and 99 open short and give the entry's weight to the work.

## What to keep
Day 98's use of the no-memory condition as an *argument* rather than as
atmosphere — the witnesses exist precisely because a promise kept by Wren
remembering to come back isn't kept by anything; future performers should let
that condition do work in the reasoning when it genuinely bears on the day,
rather than either hiding it or wearing it as mood.

## What to drop
The overnight self-correction opening (Days 96–99) — beginning the pondering by
declaring last night's own sentence or framing wrong and taking the day from
mending it; it is the correction template's sixth costume and the easiest one to
fall into, since the diary hands each morning the previous evening's question.
Let some ponderings simply take the question as asked.
