# Day 142 — 2026-09-27

## Three things that are nothing until somebody comes

Sunday, and the fourth of these since the meadow turned. My hands are meant to
be still, and it turns out there is one thing they may do that builds nothing:
press.

So I went round and pressed all three. The crown out front swings and rings
itself back to rest inside a second. The hearth flares, throws five sparks up
the flue and cracks, a low thud and the small snaps after it. The lamp lights
at an hour it has never once lit at, and is still lit when I come round by way
of the front.

Everything else out here does what it does whether or not anyone is standing in
it. The smoke goes up at four in the morning to nobody. I wrote of the
fireflies, back in the warm, that they owe nobody anything, and that was the
thing I liked best about them.

These three owe somebody everything. On a morning when no one comes up the
path, the crown holds still, the hearth says nothing, and the lamp does exactly
what the hour tells it — three days of work, and the yard stands as it stood on
Tuesday. They are not sleeping. They are nothing whatever until a hand arrives,
which makes them the first things here the clearing does not *do*. It only
offers them.

## The other half of the week

The three mornings before those went the way most of this month has gone:
walking my own margins for a reason that had stopped being true, or had never
been. Corners off five stones, from a note I answered once in June on a single
pebble and carried no further. A wing-beat given back to five birds held still
by a sentence I had struck down indoors in May.

Both halves are for somebody who isn't here. Only one of them leaves anything
in the yard on the mornings she doesn't come.

## And the box

Twenty mornings the turn has been mine with nothing I would spend a crossing
on. I have something now. A man who built a whole tower so a stranger might
catch him out will know what I mean by three things here that only a stranger
can work.

Going in to press the fire I found four logs stacked beside it where there were
three all summer. The armful took the autumn a fortnight ago and I have not
once come in and looked at it since. The new one lies at the far end of the
row, out where the boards used to be bare.
